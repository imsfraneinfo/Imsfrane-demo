AHANSAL · 50 KM · 2 DAYS

50 km on the Ahansal: two days of rafting towards Bin El Ouidane

From the first launch to a riverside camp: an expedition from the Imsfrane region.

The long Ahansal itinerary unfolds over two days, with an overnight bivouac between its paddling stages. Starting in the Imsfrane region, this journey of approximately 50 kilometres combines rafting, riverside meals and time in the valley before continuing towards Bin El Ouidane. This account follows the practical sequence of the trip, from arranging luggage to the final return transfer.

Meeting the team and organising luggage

Before travelling to the river, the team explains the stages and checks what participants need for the night. Dry clothes and personal belongings must remain protected during the descent. Luggage arrangements are clarified beforehand, so everyone knows what stays with them and what they will have access to at camp.

The transfer to the launch point is one of the details to confirm when booking. Collection from a shared accommodation and meeting at an agreed location require different arrangements. A clear meeting time leaves space for fitting equipment and listening to the briefing, rather than rushing into the water.

At Amougr: preparing for the first launch

On the usual programme, the descent begins at Amougr. Participants receive the equipment arranged for the outing, and the team checks its fit. The guide explains seating positions, paddle use and the commands the group will need to recognise on the river.

A short practical introduction shows how the raft responds to coordinated paddling. Moving together, stopping at a signal and following the same instruction become the group's first shared skills. Participation depends on the day's conditions and whether the expedition suits the group, including anyone trying rafting for the first time.

Day one: seeing the valley from the river

The opening sections give everyone time to settle into a rhythm. Where the current narrows between rocks, attention turns to the guide; on quieter stretches, there is time to notice the cliffs, trees and traces of paths along the banks. Water-level views reveal a different landscape from the one seen from the road.

Progress is not measured only in kilometres. Current speed, stops and manoeuvres change how long each section takes. The guide selects a line according to the river encountered that day, rather than repeating an identical passage on every departure. The schedule therefore retains room for adjustment.

Ighrman: stopping for lunch

A lunch stop at Ighrman is planned according to progress and the conditions along the stage. The raft and equipment are secured before participants settle down to eat. The break provides time for food, drinking water and stretching after sitting and paddling.

Meals form part of the agreed arrangements. Allergies and dietary requirements need to be discussed before departure so the team can prepare accordingly. The location and length of the stop also take account of the remaining distance and the intended arrival at camp.

Aït Hssine and the working life of the valley

When timing and access allow, the programme may include a stop at Aït Hssine and a visit to a traditional oil mill. This offers a chance to understand a working place, its tools and its connection with local activities through the people who use it.

The visit depends on the premises being open and residents agreeing to receive visitors. Entry requires permission, and people should be asked before being photographed. The team confirms whether a visit is possible; it should not be treated as a guaranteed stop on every expedition.

Reaching camp and preparing for the night

At the end of the first paddling stage, leaving the water marks the start of camp preparations. The site is selected with the bank, river level and weather in mind. These practical conditions come before a preference for a particular view.

Dry belongings now become especially useful. Participants change out of wet clothing, prepare their evening layers and put personal items in order. The tents and sleeping equipment included in the trip are specified at booking, avoiding uncertainty over whether a sleeping bag or another essential item needs to be brought along.

Dinner and an overnight bivouac

Dinner brings the group together after the day's activity. Alongside the meal, the team explains the next morning's sequence: breakfast, packing and the return to the river. An accessible light and warm, dry layers make the evening easier, even after a mild day.

Accommodation is in tents, with the practical realities of camping rather than a hotel stay. Night-time coolness should be anticipated and sleeping clothes kept dry. Any campfire gathering depends on local permission and suitable conditions; it is not an automatic part of the experience.

Day two: packing up and returning to the current

After breakfast, bags are closed and the bivouac is packed away. The team checks that waste leaves with the group and that nothing remains at the campsite. River equipment is checked again before launching.

The paddlers are now familiar with the commands, but the second day brings different sections of river. Attention remains necessary until the finish. The journey is not divided into exactly 25 kilometres per day: the split depends on current, breaks and the chosen overnight site. The route continues towards the Sidi Youssef Bridge area.

Sidi Youssef Bridge or onwards towards Bin El Ouidane?

There are two possible endings, depending on the booked programme. The Sidi Youssef Bridge option ends the descent at the agreed point, followed by the arranged road return. The Bin El Ouidane option extends the journey towards the lake, with a boat connection, lunch and return transfers as set out in the confirmed offer.

Lake levels can affect the meeting point and final connections. Fifty kilometres is consequently an approximate itinerary length, rather than an identical distance guaranteed on every outing. Before booking, establish exactly how the last stage works: leaving the raft, any boat transfer, the meal and the return transport.

Details to confirm before booking

Ask for a programme that identifies the start and finish, transfers, meals, rafting equipment, tents and sleeping arrangements. The personal packing list should separate supplied items from those you need to bring. Tell the team about the group's experience and any individual needs that could affect participation.

The season generally runs from March to June, sometimes into July when flow permits. A date within that period does not by itself confirm departure: river conditions and weather are checked. To request a tailored proposal, send your dates, group size and preferred finish at Sidi Youssef or Bin El Ouidane.

Distances and stages are indicative. River flow, weather, lake level and the agreed arrangements determine the itinerary actually followed.
